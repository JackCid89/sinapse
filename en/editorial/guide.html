§ 01

Identity

SINAPSE is a weekly magazine published in English and Spanish for STEM professionals. It covers science (papers, discoveries), technology (AI, hardware, software, quantum computing) and industry (M&A, funding, regulation). It ships every Tuesday with the previous week's activity.

§ 02

Structure of each issue

  1. Pulse — 5 to 7 short notes (~80–120 words each) with discipline tags.
  2. Cover — Long article (800–1,200 words) on the most relevant discovery or event.
  3. Papers — 3 to 5 papers with a short sheet (journal, authors, summary, "why it matters").
  4. Tech — Advances in AI, software, hardware. Short cards.
  5. Industry — Corporate moves, regulation, funding.
  6. Column — Opinion essay (500–700 words) signed by Jack Cid.
  7. Datum of the week — A striking figure with its context.
  8. Appendix — Full technical sheets of the papers (summary, methodology, limitations, link).
§ 03

Tone and editorial rules

  • No hype. No "revolutionary", "unprecedented", or "historic" without data to back it up.
  • Paper-first. Always link the primary source when possible.
  • Always context. What came before, what problem it solves, what the limitations are.
  • Rigorous, technical but accessible. Assume a reader with STEM training, not necessarily a specialist in the discipline.
  • Direct. Short sentences. Active voice. No filler.
  • Languages: English and Spanish, kept in lockstep. Both editions published the same Tuesday.
§ 04

Rotation rules

Cover

Don't repeat the same discipline as the last 2 issues. Look for variety across: physics, biology, astronomy, chemistry, engineering, earth sciences, medicine, computing, energy, behavioral sciences.

Papers

At least 2 disciplines NOT in the previous issue. Find fresh angles even within the same discipline.

Geographic plurality

Each issue must represent at least three distinct regions of the world, where regions are: North America, Europe, Asia (China, Japan, South Korea, India, Southeast Asia), Latin America, Africa, Middle East, and Oceania. Prioritize regions not covered in the two previous issues. The goal is to avoid over-representation of any region — including the Western one — without privileging any in particular. Science happens in many places; the SINAPSE archive should reflect that.

Column · rotating tones

Use a different tone than the previous issue:

  • Reflective-connective: connects two or more seemingly distant areas.
  • Critically provocative: challenges a dominant narrative.
  • Ethical-prospective: looks at implications 5–10 years out.
  • Historical-comparative: uses precedent to illuminate the present.
  • Technical-explanatory: digs into a poorly understood mechanism.
§ 05

Design and files

  • Single shared CSS at assets/sinapse.css.
  • Typography: serif (Source Serif Pro) for headings; sans-serif (Inter) for body; monospace (IBM Plex Mono) for metadata.
  • Palette: paper beige (#f6f3ee), black ink (#1a1a1a), garnet accent (#8b1a1a).
  • Maximum width: 920 px. No images (low weight, fast Pages deploy).
  • Masthead-style header with a double rule; numbered TOC; sections with "§ XX" epigraphs.
  • The Column section flips to a dark background to stand out visually.
  • The Appendix uses collapsible <details>.
§ 06

File conventions

  • Each issue (ES): issues/nXXX-[mes]-AAAA.html (XXX = sequential with zeros).
  • Each issue (EN): en/issues/nXXX-[month]-YYYY.html, same number, translated month.
  • Master registry: data/registro.json (updates every cycle; includes i18n.en for translations).
  • Shared stylesheet: assets/sinapse.css.
  • Public landings: index.html (ES, default) and en/index.html.
